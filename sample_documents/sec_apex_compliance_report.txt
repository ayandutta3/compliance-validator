Apex Financial Corp — Regulatory Compliance Assessment (FY 2026)

This report summarizes Apex Financial Corp's compliance posture against applicable
financial regulations for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2026.

1. Capital Adequacy
   As of December 31, 2026, Apex Financial Corp's capital cushion stands at 9.8% of
   total risk-weighted assets. This is below the statutory minimum floor, primarily
   attributable to loan portfolio expansion undertaken during Q3 and Q4. Management
   has initiated a capital restoration plan targeting compliance by Q2 2027.

2. Quarterly Financial Reporting
   All quarterly financial reports for Q1 through Q4 were independently audited and
   filed within the stipulated submission window. In Q3, a loan totaling $150,000 was
   extended to the Chief Operating Officer. This related-party transaction has been
   fully reported and documented in Note 14 of the consolidated financial statements.

3. Risk Governance Structure
   Risk oversight is maintained by a dedicated risk committee, which currently comprises
   two independent non-executive directors and one executive director. The committee
   meets on a quarterly cadence and reports to the Board of Directors.

4. Liquidity Management
   The current Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) is measured at 85% of projected net cash
   outflows under regulatory stress scenarios. Management has acknowledged the shortfall
   and filed a remediation plan with the relevant regulatory body.

5. Insider Trading Controls
   Apex enforces a formal insider trading policy. Mandatory blackout periods prohibit
   executive and director securities trades commencing 14 calendar days before each
   fiscal quarter-end and remain in effect until 48 hours following the public release
   of quarterly earnings.

6. Cybersecurity Incident Disclosure
   On March 4, 2026, a database intrusion affecting internal systems was detected and
   initially assessed. The incident was formally determined to be material on March 7,
   2026. A disclosure filing was compiled and submitted publicly on March 11, 2026.

7. Records Retention
   Corporate archives maintain all transaction records, accounting books, and general
   ledgers in a read-only archival system for a period of 7 years from the date of
   creation, in compliance with applicable record-keeping requirements.

8. Executive Compensation Framework
   Current executive employment contracts do not include clawback provisions for the
   recovery of incentive-based cash bonuses in the event of a financial restatement.
   A review of compensation governance is planned for the next Board cycle.
